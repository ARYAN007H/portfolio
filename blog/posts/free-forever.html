Sheet No. 04 · Standing order

Free forever is a constraint

No accounts, no paywall, no analytics. The feature that quietly decides all the others.

By Aryan 14 Sep 2026 3 min Filed under: product, principles

"Free forever" sounds like a price. It's actually a design constraint, and it's the most useful one I have.

Every project on the catalogue carries the same standing order: free, no accounts, no ads. That single sentence deletes whole categories of work before they start. No user table. No password reset flow. No email service. No billing integration. No "pro" tier hiding the good features behind a blur. No analytics dashboard telling me which button nobody presses.

People assume removing features makes a product simpler to build. It does. It also makes it smaller, and smaller is harder to fake. When there's no account, there's no growth metric. When there's no paywall, there's no funnel. You're left with the thing itself.

Nothing here has a server bill yet. That's the whole trick, and it's a fragile one. — hosting note

What it costs

Free for you is not free. It costs time, and it costs the occasional awkward conversation with a free tier. Static hosting is generous right now. The domain, if I ever buy one, is the only recurring bill. But the real cost is maintenance: twelve projects, twelve sets of dependencies, twelve chances for a link to rot.

No analytics has a cost too. I don't know which pages people read. I don't know whether anyone downloads the AppImage or just looks at the screenshot. I'm designing blind, on purpose. The alternative is a tracking script and a dashboard I'd check too often, optimizing for a number that doesn't pay rent.

I'd rather guess and keep the page light.

The alternative

I could put the good features behind a login and call the rest a free tier. I could add a "support me" banner. I could ship a trial that expires and a modal that asks for an email before the download starts. All of that is normal now. Normal is not the same as good.

The moment you add an account, you owe the user a password reset, a privacy policy, a data deletion path, and a reason to trust you with a credential. That's a lot of surface area for a chess engine. The engine doesn't need to know who you are. Neither does the gallery app. Neither does the weather app.

No accounts is not a feature I add at the end. It's the reason the rest of the app is small.

The bill

I'm 17. I don't have a company, a runway, or a plan to monetise a music player. What I have is a free tier, a laptop, and enough stubbornness to keep the links working. That's the whole business model. If it stops being possible, I'll say so, and the projects will still be open source, which is the part that actually matters.

Free forever is a promise about the user, not about me. The app is yours. The bill is mine.
